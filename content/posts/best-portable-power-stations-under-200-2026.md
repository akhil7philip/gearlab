## Critical Buying Guide: What Actually Matters Under $200

### 1. Battery Chemistry: LiFePO4 Is Non-Negotiable

Under $200, you will find two types of batteries:

| Factor | LiFePO4 | Li-ion (Li-NMC) |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| Cycle life | 2,500-4,000 cycles | 500-800 cycles |
| Lifespan (weekly use) | 10+ years | 3-5 years |
| Safety | Excellent (low fire risk) | Good (higher fire risk) |
| Weight | Slightly heavier | Lighter |
| Cost | Now standard under $200 | Slightly cheaper |

**Verdict:** Every unit in this guide uses LiFePO4. If you find a "deal" under $200 with Li-ion, skip it. The lifespan difference is 5-8x, and LiFePO4 is now cheap enough that there's no excuse.

### 2. The 80% Usable Capacity Rule

Marketing claims "240Wh" — reality delivers 190-210Wh after inverter losses. Plan for **15-20% less usable capacity** than advertised.

**Real capacity guide:**
- **99-150Wh:** 1 day of phone/laptop charging, emergency only
- **200-256Wh:** 1-2 nights camping, phone/laptop/router backup
- **300Wh+:** 2-3 nights camping, small appliance backup

### 3. Charging Speed: The Hidden Spec

A unit that charges in 1 hour vs. 5.5 hours is the difference between "charge during lunch, use it this afternoon" and "plan a day ahead." Fast charging becomes critical during extended outages when you need to cycle between grid and battery power.

| Unit | Charge Time (0-80%) | Practical Impact |
|------|---------------------|------------------|
| EcoFlow RIVER 3 | 1 hour | Charge between calls |
| Anker 521 | 2 hours | Charge during a movie |
| Bluetti AC2A | 1.5 hours | Charge over dinner |
| Jackery 240 v2 | 5.5 hours | Charge overnight |

### 4. Avoid No-Name Amazon Brands

We've tested sub-$100 "500Wh" units that measured 180-220Wh actual capacity. Worse, some lack basic battery management systems — genuine fire hazards. Under $200, stick to: **Anker, EcoFlow, Jackery, Bluetti, Goal Zero**.

> **Red flags:** No brand website, no safety certifications (UL, CE, FCC), reviews mentioning "smell" or "got hot," and capacity claims that seem too good for the price.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I run a refrigerator with a sub-$200 power station?
**No.** Full-size refrigerators draw 100-150W continuously with 600-1,200W startup surges. Mini fridges need 60-100W. The units in this guide lack both the capacity and output for meaningful refrigerator runtime. For fridge backup, you need at least 500Wh — see our [under-$500 guide](/blog/best-portable-power-stations-under-500-2026/).

### What's the difference between "under $200" and "under $300"?
Under $200 gets you 200-256Wh capacity, 200-300W output, and personal electronics coverage. Under $300 gets you 286-518Wh, 300-600W output, and small appliance capability. The jump from $200 to $300 is significant — if budget allows, the [EcoFlow RIVER 3 Plus](/blog/best-portable-power-stations-under-500-2026/) at $249 is a major upgrade.

### Are refurbished power stations worth it?
Refurbished units from Anker, Jackery, and EcoFlow are typically fine with 15-25% savings. The trade-off: shorter warranties (6-12 months vs. 2-5 years). Under $200, the savings are small ($20-40) — we recommend new for the full warranty.

### How long do budget power station batteries last?
LiFePO4 batteries (all units in this guide): 2,500-4,000+ cycles = 10+ years of weekly use. After cycle limits, capacity degrades to 60-80% of original — still usable, just shorter runtime.

### Do I need solar panels?
Not for home backup or car camping — all units charge from wall outlets. Solar makes sense for: off-grid camping, multi-day hiking, and extended outages. Solar panels add $80-200 to total cost. Under $200, solar is optional — the units are small enough to charge quickly from wall power.

### Can I use these while charging?
**Yes** — pass-through charging is standard on all units in this guide. The EcoFlow RIVER 3 also offers UPS mode: instant switchover if grid power cuts out.

### What's the best power station for CPAP under $200?

The **EcoFlow RIVER 3** is ideal for CPAP backup under $200. Its <10ms UPS switchover prevents breathing interruptions during power transitions. Runtime: 5-6 hours without humidifier, 3-4 hours with. For multi-night backup, you'll need a larger unit — see our [dedicated CPAP guide](/blog/best-portable-power-station-cpap-2026/).

### Can I take these on a plane?

Only the **Jackery Explorer 100 Plus** (99Wh) is FAA-compliant for carry-on without airline approval. All other units in this guide exceed the 100Wh limit and require airline permission — which most airlines deny for power stations. Check our [travel guide](/blog/best-portable-power-stations-2026/) for detailed TSA and international airline policies.
